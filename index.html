Model notes

World model glacial-0.8: rigid plates on 100,002 geodesic cells drive relief; a seasonal zonal energy-balance model with ocean mixed-layer and land heat capacity (so seasons are damped by the sea and strongest deep inland), a prescribed three-cell wind pattern that follows the seasons, and moisture transport across the mesh (carried by the mean winds and spread poleward by storm-track eddies) produce temperature, rain shadows, Köppen climates and Whittaker-style biomes. Runoff from a Budyko water balance drains over the terrain into rivers, lakes and drainage basins; lakes that evaporate more than they receive become salt lakes with closed basins. The ice-age view replays a glacial maximum: lower obliquity, glacial greenhouse gases and dust cool the energy-balance model, ice sheets grow from snowfall and melt over a 90,000-year build-up, and the sea falls by the volume of the new ice, exposing shelves and land bridges. Conceptual approximations, not a validated Earth-system model.
